PRACTICE · THE LIVE VIEW
Three instruments on one strip
One pass for #1364 (a session timer), #1367 (the pass counter) and #1499 (which beats the click sounds, and the time signature that makes that mean something). They ship as separate PRs, smallest first, but they compete for the same screen, so they are designed together. Spec: specs/practice-instruments.md.
The constraint
The screen is already full. With the pass counter on, today's Focus Player runs to the bottom edge. So the budget was spent deliberately rather than shared out: the session timer is resident because it is orientation and costs no row; the pass counter is opt-in and costs nothing when off; the click's pattern goes two layers down and leaves only an honest indicator behind. The resting screen is unchanged from what shipped, which is the test T2 and T14 set.

Copy · open
Clean and Fluffed
Clean is what pianists already say about a pass and it stays. Missed was the weak half: you miss a note, not a pass, and it describes you rather than the playing. Fluffed is its exact British counterpart, describes the pass, and carries no shame. Alternatives worth a look: Clean / Not clean (perfectly symmetrical, a little clinical), Solid / Scrappy (two adjectives, both about the playing), Got it / Not yet (warm, but edges toward the app encouraging you). Header is Passes, not Repetitions.
 
What is deliberately not here
No accent timbre on the downbeat. A second click sound is a separate question from which beats sound at all, and mixing them would make this round untestable by ear.

No subdivisions. The grid is beats in the bar. Playing quavers against a crotchet click is a tempo question, not a pattern one.

No bar counting. The indicator says where you are in the bar, never which bar.

No flash. At the top of the tempo range (208bpm, 3.5Hz) a large high-contrast flash crosses WCAG's general flash threshold, and stops being legible anyway.
Settled here
  • The tempo number never changes meaning. A sparse pattern does not divide the bpm; it gates which beats of the same grid sound. So achieved_tempo keeps meaning what T16 says it means, and a click on beat 4 at 120 evidences 120.
  • The row must never imply every beat when it is not. The dots are the honesty mechanism, and they are the same argument T14 made about putting a marking back on a row whose next tap sounds something else.
  • The metre is offered with an answer already in it. Asking a musician mid-session for a time signature is the admin friction T1 says to remove; showing the one the app already holds, editable, is not.
  • “Passes”, not “Repetitions”. Jon's own words on #1367, and a musician's (tone rule 2). Clean and Missed stay: they are already right, and a miss stays taupe rather than red.
  • Hitting the target is not congratulated. Tone V1: the app does not know how it went. The dots filling is the acknowledgement.
  • No new tokens. Everything here is catalogue: the click capsule, rep slot dots, SegmentedPills for the presets, BottomSheet, CardSurface. The beat grid is the one new primitive, and it is rep slot dots given a number and a tap target.
Left to the spec, not decided by drawing
  • Whether ChordChart.metre stays authoritative with a piece-level fallback, or moves up to the piece and the chart derives from it. The sheet renders the same either way.
  • Whether the metre carries a denominator, which is what 6/8 needs to group 3+3.
  • Whether the pattern is recorded next to the tempo. The design assumes yes, because the trend chart (T17) would otherwise draw “120 straight” and “120 on beat 4” as the same point.
