Why A won
  • One primary action. B shows two start buttons a thumb apart, both of which begin a session. A has one, and the escape hatch reads as an escape hatch.
  • B repeats itself. The header subtitle says "Last practised Tuesday" and B's hero says the same thing again, in bigger type, three lines below. The suggested piece is usually the last piece too, so the title lands twice.
  • Content over chrome. A's hero is the user's own pieces and marks. B's hero is a play glyph.
  • Nothing is lost. Last practised keeps its line in the subtitle, which is where tone of voice already decided it belongs.
What A costs
  • It changes a canonical pillar screen, so the design system's Practice screen is updated in the same pass and this file shrinks to the journey.
  • The hero has two shapes to snapshot rather than one: suggestion, and the shipped play-glyph hero for frame C.
  • The 96pt play button goes. It is the most distinctive thing on the tab today.
Settled, either way
  • Exercises first, the piece last, matching how the builder already lays out a block.
  • Every row carries its reason, and the reasons come from the core. The screen never composes one.
  • The step rides with its exercise ("step F"), because the ladder's current rung is what gets practised.
  • "Build my own instead" is secondary and its result is frame C: the screen that shipped.
  • No new tokens and no new components. Card surface, eyebrow, type badges, hero gradient and the brand button are all catalogue parts.
