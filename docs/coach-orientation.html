Where we are, in five numbers

The pivot was decided on 19 July. Since then: the dead shells are gone, the capture harness and segmentation spike landed and closed Phase 1 early, and the newest ruling — 4 August — is that machine listening waits. The coaching loop ships first on tap-verdicts.

The one-sentence version, updated. You bring the goal ("improvise confidently on jazz standards"); the app decides what you practise today, sets the click, the key and the schedule, and — for now — asks you to confirm the pass against a countable criterion rather than listening itself. It still tells you when you're done and quietly makes the next attempt winnable when you're stuck. The microphone returns later, playing a lick in rather than typing it, once the loop has proven it retains.

What actually changed

Same app, same piano, opposite direction of information flow. The notebook asked you to tell it what happened. The coach tells you what to do and finds out for itself.

Before — the practice notebook

  • You built the session: pick pieces, drag blocks into order
  • You ran a stopwatch and counted your own reps
  • You rated yourself 0–10 afterwards and typed a reflection
  • Analytics turned your own opinions into mastery dials
  • Progress meant "I logged 20 minutes"

After — the practice coach

  • It builds the session before you sit down. One screen, one button
  • It sets the terms — click, key, scope, schedule — and asks you to confirm the pass against a countable criterion (a tap-verdict, until the mic returns)
  • Gates, not minutes: "3 clean passes at 120" releases the block
  • Mastery is measured evidence, held as an estimate plus a confidence
  • Progress means "the gate opened, and here's the trend"
the honest boundary — narrower than it was

What the app is authoritative about, today

Tempo, key, scope and schedule — the parameters it sets and you can't fudge. Getting better is the levels climbing, and the levels are machine-facts even though no microphone is listening yet. See "No ears yet".

What still waits for the microphone

Right notes, per-bar timing, swing ratio — the vision's "listens while you play" is real but deferred (decision 18). Touch, feel and phrasing were always self-judged and stay so.

Three rulings you should know about — now scoped to the path they'll govern

RulingWhy it exists
Never correct in real time A mid-phrase flash pulls your eyes to the screen, which ruins the next phrase, which flashes again. Holds today too — the app is silent while you play; the tap-verdict lands between reps, same as a machine verdict would.
Timing is judged on consistency, never on deviation Skilled jazz timing sits off the grid on purpose — a stable 30ms lay-back reads as swinging harder. Only wobble and drift are errors. Waits for MIDI: nothing measures timing deviation without it.
The input path decides what may be claimed Wired MIDI earns fine timing feedback; Bluetooth (±10–20ms jitter) gets notes, swing and trends only, and the screen says why. Waits for MIDI: v1 has one input path — you, tapping.

Decision 18 — no ears yet, and the loop ships anyway

Recorded 4 August, after the segmentation spike came back with findings rather than a green light. The scoring path — MIDI capture, attempt segmentation, per-bar verdicts — needs machine-readable, note-level, per-key targets for everything scorable. That's content that has to be sourced or typed in: exactly the admin friction this whole design exists to remove. And an app optimises what it can see — a scoring-first build pulls the product toward a drill machine for the narrow slice of material that can be scored, which is the opposite of the vision.

What actually changes. Not the vision — "listens while you play" is still the destination. What moves is the order: the retention-critical journeys (1, 5, 6, 7 — first session, circling, the bad day, coming back) need none of the microphone. So the coaching loop ships first on the evidence model below, and the segmentation spike's engine module stays merged and inert, ready for when scoring returns.

Two insights that make coaching work without a verdict from a microphone

The parameters are machine-facts even when the pass is human

The app still sets the click, prescribes the key, the scope and the schedule. "Passed at 96 in D♭, hands together" contains exactly one subjective bit — the pass. Getting better is the levels climbing, and the levels are objective whether or not anything is listening.

Measure what survived, not what happened today

Motor consolidation makes in-session judgement lie in both directions — next-day retention, not in-session polish, is what separates effective practisers (Duke, Simmons & Cash 2009). So the headline instrument is the cold test: the first rep of the day on returning material, flagged as such, no questions first — count-in, play, one tap. A pass after a gap is the strongest evidence available without listening.

The evidence tiers

TierWhatFeeds
Machine-fact Tempo at pass, keys covered, scope, intervals survived, time-by-circle, rep counts Mastery parameters, the circling check, the tally
Tap-verdict Pass/fail on a countable criterion; attempts-to-pass; the cold-test result; where-it-stopped (a tap on a bar map, when asked) Mastery (decision 17 as amended); the circling check
Judgement One-tap feel rating; voice or text reflections; opaque-target retirement The judgement track only — trends, retirement, never a prerequisite
Completion Off-piano queue items: did it happen or didn't Streak-equivalent, the off-piano tally

Every evidence record carries a source tag — TapVerdict now, Midi / Audio later at higher weight — so machine scoring arrives as an upgrade to the same Beta update, never a migration. The asymmetry from decision 17 stands unchanged: the judgement track may retire a target and may never satisfy a prerequisite. What moves is only who counts the passes — and counting is self-correcting in a single-player app, because a generous pass just buys a prescription that's too hard tomorrow.

The re-entry trigger, recorded now so it isn't forgotten. When the loop retains and users start asking "did I actually nail it?", MIDI returns — play-to-input first: play the lick in once, the capture becomes the target, and the existing chart-transposition code turns it into all twelve keys automatically. Thirty seconds of playing replaces the note-entry admin that made scoring content expensive in the first place. Verification against a machine-scored target is the second step, not the first.

What's deliberately still not measured

  • Minutes as a headline. Quantity explains roughly a quarter of outcome variance. Small print, never celebrated.
  • Honesty. No lie detector exists here. The blind spot is accepted, recorded, and is the door machine listening re-enters through.
  • Tempo as a target in itself. Gates say clean at a tempo, the cold test confirms it stuck, and the feel track can veto the number — "hit 96, felt terrible" has to be a state the app can represent.

One boundary worth being precise about: ear training, if built, is fully machine-scorable without listening — the app plays the question, the answer is a screen tap. "No machine verdicts in v1" is a consequence of deferring the input path, not a dogma about what the app can check.

Who decides what you practise

Added to the spec today (v3), because the earlier draft couldn't answer an obvious question: if the app kills the choice, how does it know what you want to work on now? The answer had been "you bring the goal", which left the altitude people actually plan at — the week or fortnight — with nowhere to live.

The rule. You steer the planner's inputs; you don't hand-edit its output. Prescription isn't choice denied — it's choice relocated to the moment you had the information to make it well, instead of at the piano, tired, with the whole taxonomy in view.

Three altitudes, all optional

You sayHorizonExampleIf you say nothing
The goal Months "Improvise confidently over standards" The graph's own frontier
A campaign 1–3 weeks "Restricted improv over Strasbourg" — or a whole lesson's worth of targets Back-chained from the goal
Today's steer This session "More transcription"; "hands only, I'm tired" Whatever the campaign makes due

Naming a target isn't writing a plan

Say "learn the tune, transcribe, then improvise over it" and you haven't authored a sequence — you've described what the app already knows. The tune pipeline orders form → melody → shells → rootless. The graph knows chord-tone targeting needs shells first, because you can't aim at the 3rds and 7ths until you know where they live.

You choose the what; the graph supplies the order. That's the whole reason a granular session builder can be dropped without dropping control.

It back-chains, and it argues

Declare restricted improv when your shells are weak and it neither obeys nor refuses. It works backwards to the prerequisite and tells you the distance in your own trend: "then we start with shells — roughly five sessions from here."

Honour the destination, own the route, be honest about the gap. A planner that just does as it's told is a worse teacher than one that pushes back.

A lesson is a set, not a single target

The real case is a handful of things at once: work the voicings, transcribe this phrase, get the bridge from memory. So a campaign holds several targets. Each is matched to a skill node or pipeline stage where it can be — and where it can't, it's kept as-is rather than forced into an ill-fitting node.

"Make the last A sing" isn't a node. It gets practised and self-confirmed, not scored. And when targets like it keep recurring, that's the queue telling you which node to author next — content driven by demand, not by filling in a taxonomy.

The gap read — two tiers, never blurred

Once you've named targets, "what's missing?" becomes arithmetic rather than a feeling.

  • Structural — unmet prerequisites, pipeline stages you're behind. Pure graph maths, trustworthy on day one.
  • Statistical — confidence that's decayed, drills gone flat, a starved circle. Needs weeks of history first.

Show the first immediately, withhold the second until the data earns it, and present either as a route rather than a verdict.

And one thing the design forbids. No "guided vs my own way" switch. Asking which mode you're in at the door reintroduces exactly the decision the app exists to remove. It always opens prescribed, and yields in one tap.

Where AI belongs in this — and where it doesn't

Destination: good fit

"I want to sound like Bill Evans on ballads" → candidate nodes, tunes and phrases. Fuzzy, linguistic, cheap to check, and you can edit the answer. An LLM is the right tool.

Today's route: deliberately not

Every block owes you a why that cites real state. A generated plan can't be tested, and one arbitrary-feeling prescription sends you back to noodling. The planner stays arithmetic.

Guiding without nagging

Added today (v4) after a critical review of the whole design. It found one root fault: the app managed failure thoroughly and everything else barely. Three fixes, all aimed at the states that aren't failure.

was missing

Fixation

Every intervention fired on failure — three fails shrinks the task, three flat sessions forces a change. Nothing noticed someone succeeding and unable to leave. Grinding one voicing for a month is failure-free and expensive.

was missing

Knowing where you are

"Gates, not minutes" is right pedagogically and rough on anyone whose time sense is unreliable. An open-ended "until 3 clean passes" with no visible bound is how twenty minutes becomes fifty.

was too thin

The pull of grind

"Honest about the grind" is respect, not a mechanism. A plan that needs willpower to get through twelve keys loses to any brain that runs on interest rather than importance.

The circling check — the mirror of the stuck ladder

The stuck ladder makes the task smaller when you're failing. This makes you stop when you're burrowing. Both fire on their own; a teacher does both without being asked.

What it noticesFrom data the session already has
You can't leave a drill that's already passedReps after the gate opened
A perfectionism loopAttempts-to-pass far above your own norm
One thing eating the fortnightShare of session time per skill
A campaign that never landsSessions elapsed vs the horizon you declared
Always wandering to the same placeRepeat frequency in the wander log
Novelty churn — the opposite problemCampaigns declared and dropped inside a week

Framing matters more than detection. Once per session at most, at a block boundary or the end — never mid-rep, because that's both a verdict and an interruption. Phrased as an observation plus permission, offering the exit rather than demanding it: "You've run this fourteen times and passed on the fourth. It's in. Bank it and move?"

Never "you're stuck on this", and never a tally of wasted minutes. Fixation usually arrives with shame already attached — an app that adds to it gets closed.

Orientation is not feedback

The fix for hidden time isn't a dashboard, it's a distinction. Verdicts stay rationed; where you are never is. Elapsed, blocks left, and this block's ceiling sit ambient on the drill screen carrying no judgement. The one thing that never appears there is a score.

Three structural moves on grind

  • Capped shorter than a conventional plan would set it — two brief exposures beat one long one, and the planner already interleaves.
  • Never in the abstract. Always over the tune's real changes. The same voicings over music you love are a different experience, and the engine already supports it — now it's a hard constraint, not a preference.
  • Explicitly tradeable. "Skip the grind today, two blocks tomorrow" as a logged deal. Far better than the silent skip that becomes three weeks off.

And four smaller corrections

The voice talks less

Five blocks meant five coaching moments per session. Now one, where it lands hardest, plus on demand. Silence at a boundary is the common case.

Off-piano work gets its own queue

Listening and audiation were modelled as session blocks, so they'd be done at the piano — wasting the one thing they don't need — or not at all. Separate queue, own cue, commute-sized.

Sometimes nothing is watching

Off-piste still listens and logs. Below it now: unmonitored play — time logged, nothing scored, nothing inferred. A tool you can't play without being measured isn't a practice room.

Rhythm needs to be a real subject

It existed only as a modifier inside other skills — odd for a design whose foundation is time. Time gets measured thoroughly and taught nowhere. Now first in the authoring queue.

One thing the review got wrong, worth recording. I argued the design couldn't express rubato and needed an unscored feel category. That was overstated: playing against a steady pulse is swing — lay-back, the ratio, playing ahead are all defined relative to a grid — and the app already scores consistency rather than deviation precisely so you can play against the beat without being marked wrong. Rubato belongs after time is internalised. What survived was a much smaller fix: click-always governs scored attempts, not the learning rungs beneath a gate, which the method packs were already prescribing without a click.

The two circles — your teacher's frame, now in the model

Music is the overlap of what you can hear and what your hands can execute. Fluency is that overlap growing. Either circle alone gives you exercises or frustration. Every piece of content is now tagged with which circle it feeds.

Where the first five skills sit

  • hands Shell voicings through ii–V–I
  • hands Rootless voicings, A and B positions
  • bridge Phrase transposition — a lifted lick through 12 keys
  • bridge Chord-tone targeting — land the 3rd or 7th on every change
  • head Micro-transcription — lifting 1–2 bars by ear

The risk this frame exposes. The planner rotates work by what's due, so nothing notices a starved circle. You could pass gates for months with 90% of your time in the hands circle — and measurable-first content leans exactly that way. The fix (a time-by-circle view plus a planner bias) is cheap once session data exists, and is booked for Phase 4.

The engine: four moving parts

None of this appears in the interface. The app thinks about all of it so you think about none of it — you see one drill and one reason. Intent (above) is the fifth part: the graph says what exists and in what order, the pipelines say what's in flight, and intent says where you're pointing.

1 · The skill graph

Jazz as a dependency graph, not a list. Each node knows what it is, why it matters, what must come first, its 2–4 drills, and the criteria that mean "done". Mastery is never a tick-box — it's an estimate with a confidence, and the confidence decays while you don't practise.

2 · Pipelines

Tunes and phrases are instances moving through stages, with a gate at each stage. One phrase in flight at a time, one or two tunes — the caps are the anti-overwhelm feature.

the current phrase, stage 2 of 7

  • doneLearn it in the home key
  • nowNearby keys — a whole step either side
  • thenFull cycle → analyse → extract the device
  • thenGenerate your own → deploy it over the tune

3 · Gates as data, not code

Every threshold lives in a file you can tune: how many clean passes, at what tempo, on which click level, how many fails trigger help. They ship as guesses and get calibrated from your own logs. 

[gates.phrase-nearby-keys]
criterion  = "clean in both
  whole-step neighbours,
  resolution lands on beat 1"
clean_passes  = 2
per_key       = true
tempo_bpm     = 90
click_level   = "l1"

4 · Method packs — the teacher's craft

Not what to practise but how to attack it: the ladder up (hands separate, one key slow, then the change, then tempo), the key order (whole steps to learn, cycle of fourths for fluency, random to prove it), one thinking cue per stage, and the schedule that says 2–3 new keys a session — never "all twelve".

The stuck ladder is this recipe read backwards. Anyone can list drills; the packs are the moat.

Something comfortable, then something hard, then something new, then something musical. Effort in the middle, music at both ends — that shape is a hard rule, not a preference.

When the app is allowed to speak

Organised by when, not by what. This is the difference between a coach and a backseat driver.

The click, and a passive bar-position indicator. No wrong-note flashes, no score counting up. Your ears own this time.

1

Scoped by decision 18: until the microphone returns, the tick is your tap — one per rep, and the count-in follows it. The one fact is a fact the app owns (gate progress, attempts against your own norm), never a claim about notes it can't hear. Gate progress ticks up . Per-note facts like "2 wrong notes" or "rushing bars 3–4" come back with machine listening.

The coaching voice — and it earns its slot.

The only place it speaks in sentences: why this drill, the trend, and exactly one thing to listen for next. Not every boundary — one per session, where it lands hardest, plus on demand. Five coaching moments in twenty minutes is a lot of talking from something designed not to nag.

3

The narrative.

Trends, and the thread — "Monday's voicing drill is what your left hand just did in bar 5" — then tomorrow's draft. Readable now or in the morning.

Three anti-nag rules

  • One bad rep is not an event. A cross and a fact. No "try again!", no tip. Next count-in.
  • Help acts, it doesn't narrate. Three fails and the app quietly drops the tempo or shrinks the scope, framed as the plan — "let's take it to 100" — never as remediation.
  • Name a wall once. "Everyone's enclosures sound mechanical for three weeks; here's the smaller version." The second time the same encouragement appears, it has curdled into nagging.

Feedback still thins out as a drill matures — every rep while it's new, every few reps once it's solid — but the top of the ladder, predict-then-reveal (self-rate, then see the machine's score), has nothing to reveal until the microphone is back. It's deferred with decision 18, not cut: the tap-verdict loop just has no fade schedule to graduate into yet.

Every scenario, with the mechanism that serves it and — the part that makes it testable — the failure it exists to prevent. Journeys 1, 5, 6 and 7 are the retention-critical ones. An app that only handles journey 2 is a scorer, not a coach.

What a second review found still open

The first review fixed how the app behaves. This one looked at whether the pieces hang together — and found the problems were structural rather than behavioural. One is now resolved; three are tracked as issues.

important

Five polite features are still five interruptions

The stuck ladder, the circling check, the grind trade, the coach voice and the gap read each own a "once per session" moment. Nothing arbitrates between them. This is exactly how a careful design becomes naggy anyway — and no single feature's tests would ever catch it. Needs one budget and a priority order, decided in one place.

resolved

What counts as mastery — settled

The blocking question was whether your own verdict counts toward how good the app thinks you are. Answer: no — only what it can hear. Self-report moves to a separate judgement track that records completion and a feel trend, and its power is asymmetric on purpose: it can retire a target ("the last A sings now") but never unlock a prerequisite. If your own verdict could open the next door, generosity gets quietly rewarded and the gate stops meaning anything.

Still open: the shape of the mastery function itself. Its inputs are now fixed, which was the part that needed a human decision.

worth settling

Week one has no baseline

Every adaptive mechanism needs history. In your first fortnight there's no attempts-to-pass norm, so the circling check can't say "above your norm"; no trend, so horizons in sessions aren't available. Needs an explicit list of what's suppressed and when each switches on.

worth settling

Two questions about endings

A campaign has a horizon but no definition of done, so "improvise confidently over Strasbourg" can run forever. And the session narrative — the most motivating content in the app — lands after twenty minutes of focus, when attention is spent. End on the feeling; leave the report for the morning.

And the honest scheduling finding: Phase 2 had quietly become undeliverable. It accumulated twelve deliverables under a three-to-four week estimate — planner, three declaration surfaces, back-chaining, gap read, circling check, grind trade, off-piano queue, unmonitored mode, circle tally, soft-landing exit, and deleting the session builder. Now split: 2a is the loop working end to end, 2b is everything that makes it kind. A phase that can't land is worse than a phase that admits its size.

Three decisions inside that diagram worth understanding

MIDI crosses the bridge in batches, never note by note

Every event through the Rust core rebuilds the whole view model. Piping note-ons through that at playing speed would be architecture abuse. So the Swift side owns the buffer and timestamps against the click; the core scores batches. Few, small, stable types cross the bridge — that wire has bitten us before, silently.

The planner runs on your phone, not on a server

The design doc originally put planning behind the API. The review overruled it: the practice room is the worst place to need a server round-trip, and the device database is already the source of truth. The cloud keeps only the LLM voice.

The real risk is gravity, not architecture

The old abstractions are right there, so the temptation is to hang attempt scores off the old self-rating fields and extend the builder instead of replacing it. Six months of that and the old model is wearing the new one as a costume. The guard: the engine gets its own state, never touches the self-report fields, and the pull request that lands the planner deletes the builder in the same series.

session A · start here

The drill loop — four screens

Home (plus its first-run state), during play, after a repetition (plus the cross state and the uncertain "that sounded clean — agree?" variant), and the block boundary (plus the silent boundary). These are seen every day and must be right.

session B · after A

Declaration and guard rails — three screens

Declaring a campaign from a lesson list, the route-and-gap answer, and the circling check. These inherit the voice Session A establishes, which is why they wait.

The brief carries what the old prompt here was missing: the user's moment for each screen, the failure mode to avoid, the required states (first run, empty, uncertain, failure), Dynamic Type at the largest accessibility size for the drill screen, the light-theme-only ruling, and an explicit list of what not to design.
