Turn 2 · More light-ground options

Six ways to place the swipe

All on the paper ground with brown ink. The variable is how the butter marker relates to the glyph — behind it, beneath it, filling it, or doing the drawing itself.
2aUnderline
Swipe strictly beneath the glyph, slightly tilted like a hand-drawn mark. Never overlaps the keys — same fix works for dark.
2bFilled keys
Butter fills the keyboard body instead of sitting behind it. No separate swipe shape; the glyph is the accent.
2cFull-bleed swipe
One broad marker stroke edge-to-edge behind the glyph. Bolder, closer to the Welcome animation's swipe.
2dOne key pressed
A single white key highlighted in butter — the marker marks the note you're practising. Smallest accent, most story.
2eOn the staff
Five faint staff lines cross the ground; the glyph sits on them like a highlighted bar. Ties to the Welcome staff-line reveal.
2fButter ground
Marker colour becomes the whole tile, paper glyph on top. Most visible on a busy homescreen; least "paper" of the set.
